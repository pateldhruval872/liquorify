|---|---|---|

**What this shows, and its limits.** The server stops serving an unlisted product at once. The storefront does not: it caches each product request for ten minutes (`"use cache: remote"` with `cacheLife("tenMinutes")` in `src/lib/data/products.ts`), and nothing clears the cache when a product changes, so a shopper may see an unlisted product for up to ten minutes (B19). A shopper who opened the product could also try to buy it in that time; the check did not try, and the cart's behaviour for an unlisted product is batch 9's. The not-found page the storefront then shows is answered with status 200, as batch 4 found ([part 2's B1](2026-10-26-fb-19-batch-storefront-shop-part-2.md#5-the-storefronts-behaviour-found-recorded-and-not-changed)), so the check reads the page and the Store API's status beside it rather than the storefront's status. The check ran once, for one channel, the default; a product listed on a second channel and read with that channel's key was not checked.

**The first attempt.** An earlier run of the same check, at 05:23, failed with the Store API answering 404 while the product was listed, because the browser-test database had been emptied under it (§8). It passed once the database was rebuilt; its answers are the ones above.

## 9. Limitations

- **Diagnostic, not acceptance.** A passing comparison only shows that both sides could be captured; fidelity is the owner's judgement from the evidence.
- **Invented data in stories, the browser tests' data and Spree's sample catalog on screens.** Names, counts and records differ on purpose.
- **Seeded data.** The extra products, files, metadata, role and teammate were added to the browser-test database through the Admin API. They exist only in that database, which is local and discarded, and Spree's tests and fixtures are unchanged. Spree's sample data stops before its sample orders, as it did for lane A, so no screen shows a product's orders; part 1's screens do not need them.
- **The database was rebuilt once during the run** (§8). The data before and after is the same by construction, and each capture's note names the revision it ran from; two captures, FL-04 and the final comparison ran after the rebuild.
- **States reached by answering a request** show how the screen renders that answer, not that the server would send it (§2.1).
- **The storefront check ran once,** for one product on the default channel, against a production build on one machine. The ten-minute cache is the storefront's code; how long a shopper sees an unlisted product depends on when the product was last read.
- **The metadata card's keys are masked in the comparison** on both sides, so their text is not compared; the captures show them.
- **Spree's specs ran on SQLite, locally.** CI runs Spree's suites on PostgreSQL and MySQL, and six examples run on PostgreSQL only.
- **Size.** Part 1's 508 reference images are 10.0 MB, and its evidence is 22 MB, more than any earlier batch, because the product page and its sheets are tall. The evidence images are lossless WebP.
- **Reference images from one processor.** The reference images were made in the pinned image on an Intel Xeon. Every GitHub runner whose processor was logged was an AMD EPYC machine, and these shade the ends of one story's rounded chips slightly differently (§8). With §4.3's timing corrections, no other part 1 story differed between them.
- **Not independent.** The session that built the stories also compared them; the owner's review is the independent check.
